## Ruptures: what the detector finds without looking for it

The break detector is given no date in advance. Even so, across all its configurations, it finds the same years again and again: 1975 is the most frequent break in three of the four configurations; 1958, 2004 and 2016–2018 appear in all of them, and 1994 in three of the four. The crisis of the mid-1970s, the recomposition of the 1990s and an inflection in the second half of the 2010s, followed by the robust rise of 2020–2025, are the tremors that the seismograph records most regularly.

This does not by itself validate any periodisation. It does, however, offer a test for the hypothesis of stages of real subsumption: the breaks that the theory proposes can be compared with those that the detector finds without looking for them. The 2016–2018 inflection deserves particular attention, because it coincides with the expansion of the platform economy and with massive investment in artificial intelligence.
