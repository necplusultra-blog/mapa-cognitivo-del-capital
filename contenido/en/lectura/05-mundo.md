## The world rate of profit: the fall is concentrated where accumulation is greatest

With the Penn World Table, the net world rate of profit (total economy, capital-weighted, panel of 79 countries since 1960) fluctuates between 7.6% and 9.1% from 1960 to 2007 and falls to 5.2% in 2023. Without China, the fall is smaller: to 6.0%. Over the same period, China goes from holding 2% of world capital to holding 27%, and the United States from 39% to 13%. The world mass of gross profit, meanwhile, is multiplied almost twelvefold between 1960 and 2023.

The reading is consistent with that of the two previous sections. The most intense phase of the fall coincides with the most rapid accumulation in the history of capital, concentrated in the region that incorporated the most labour power into capitalist production. The fall in the rate does not occur where capital stagnates, but where it expands most: not because labour becomes less productive, but because it becomes more productive (Marx, 1968, p. 439). That the mass keeps growing at the same time shows that the movement is not one of exhaustion, but of reconstitution of the capital relation on a broader material basis and with a higher composition.
