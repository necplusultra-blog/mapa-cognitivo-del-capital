## Artificial intelligence: appropriation at the centre of accumulation

Eight corporations —Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Nvidia, Oracle, Broadcom and AMD— appropriated in 2025 18.6% of US corporate profits and around 1.3% of the world mass of gross profit. Their operating profit is equivalent to 53% of their net fixed capital, and their investment to 37% of that capital in a single year and to 9.8% of all US private non-residential investment. Without them, the rate of the non-financial sector would be 19.8% instead of 22.5%.

These figures do not measure surplus value produced by these firms. They measure surplus value appropriated. Artificial intelligence today operates as a general condition of capitalist production (Dyer-Witheford, Kjøsen and Steinhoff, 2024, p. 55; Jiménez Cea, 2026), and whoever monopolises that condition —computing capacity, chips, models— intercepts a growing portion of the mass of surplus value produced elsewhere, just as landed property intercepts a growing part of surplus value without producing it (Marx, 2021, pp. 820–822). At the same time, the investment rate of these firms expresses the other side of the countertendency of the cheapening of constant capital: an accelerated accumulation at the highest point of the composition, which reduces the unit value of computing for the rest of capital and, at the same time, raises the mass of capital needed to compete. The extreme concentration of this industry is at once a result of the tendential fall of the rate of profit and one of the means by which capital tries to counteract it.
