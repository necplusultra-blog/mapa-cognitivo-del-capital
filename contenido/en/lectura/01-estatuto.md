## What can be asked of a measurement

National accounts do not record surplus value, variable capital or productive labour. They record prices, wages, jobs and stocks valued in money. Translating them into the categories of the critique of political economy requires theoretical decisions, and that is why the seismograph does not offer *a* rate of profit but a multiverse of specifications in which each fork corresponds to an explicit decision. Whatever withstands all the decisions can be read as a manifestation of the dynamics of capital; whatever changes with them indicates where the theory decides the result.

This caution has a deeper foundation than statistical prudence. If the immanent limits of capital were a ceiling, a well-made measurement could say how close the system is to reaching it. But the limit that the capital relation cannot cross —the reduction to zero of the labour necessary for the reproduction of the working class— has the form of an asymptote: capital approaches it without being able to reach it, because reaching it would abolish the relation itself (Marx, 2018, p. 741; 2016a, pp. 282–284). No statistical series, then, measures a ‘distance’ to the limit. What can be observed is the form in which capital processes its limits today: with what rates of exploitation, with what masses of labour, with what composition of capital and with what countertendencies. The series show the phenomenal manifestation of these determinations; they do not demonstrate them.
