## Broad surplus value (macro-monetary reading)

<div class="f">pv = VA<sub>business</sub> − VA<sub>rented housing</sub> − SF<sub>households</sub> − v</div>

- **VA<sub>business</sub>**: gross value added of the business sector in the national accounts (`A195RC`).
- **VA<sub>rented housing</sub>**: value added of housing (`A2009C`) minus that of households (`B701RC`), which corresponds to owner-occupied housing and also to the compensation of domestic service. It is excluded because it is rent, not capitalist production. Since `B701RC` includes domestic service, the subtraction falls about 25 billion dollars short (about 0.2% of pv in 2025).
- **SF<sub>households</sub>**: financial services that households pay for out of their wages (secondary exploitation; author's decision, 24 Nov. 2026): personal consumption expenditure on financial services (`DFNLRC`: implicit interest and fees) × compensation / personal income (`A033RC` / `A065RC`). It is surplus value shifted from wages to financial capital, not surplus value produced; it is subtracted in all three variants of v, including a, which is therefore not exactly the conventional measure; it is recorded separately as an indicator (section ‘What counts as surplus value’ on the main page).
- **v**: compensation of productive labour according to the chosen variant.

pv includes profits, interest, rents, taxes on production and unproductive wages, which are deductions from the total mass of surplus value. The rate of surplus value is e = pv/v. In the net variant, the depreciation of the business sector's non-residential capital is subtracted: private non-residential consumption of fixed capital, at current cost, minus that of non-profit institutions and households. The US measurements in hours (working day, mass of surplus labour, productive forces) use the net e.

Following Moseley (2016), constant and variable capital are taken as given money magnitudes (actual prices). That is why there is no need to ‘transform’ values into prices in order to measure the aggregate rate.
