## World scale

**Rate (Penn World Table 11.0).**

<div class="f">gross = (1 − labsh) · cgdpo / cn · net = ((1 − labsh) · cgdpo − δ · cn) / cn · IRR: real internal rate of return from the PWT</div>

Countries are weighted by capital stock (cn) or by GDP (cgdpo), with and without China, in a panel balanced from 1960 or unbalanced. The gross mass of profit in dollars is Σ (1 − labsh) · real GDP. The PWT measures the total economy: it does not distinguish productive from unproductive labour.

**World mass in hours.** It applies the same industry criterion as in point 3, country by country, and sums. There are three stretches of sources; all three use the same definitions.

**Block B adjustments in the central measurement (author's decision, 24 Nov. 2026).** To each country's surplus value is added the rent that market industries pay for premises and offices (intermediate consumption of real-estate products L68: surplus value produced and ceded to landed property, B3), and from it are subtracted the financial services that households pay for out of their wages (final consumption of K64–K66 × labour share of income, PWT labsh: surplus value displaced from wages, B4), both as a proportion of market value added according to the WIOD input-output tables (after 2014, 2014 proportions): e' = e + (B3 − B4) · (1 + e + cp/v). The column `e_sin_ajuste_b` of `mundo_horas_paises.csv` keeps the rate without these adjustments.

**(a) 2000–2014: WIOD, Socio-Economic Accounts 2016** (43 countries; Taiwan is excluded because of anomalies). For each country:

- **Unproductive industries (circulation):** trade (G45–G47), finance and insurance (K64–K66), legal, accounting and management services (M69–M70), advertising (M73).
- **Outside capitalist production:** real estate (L68, rent), public administration (O84), households as employers (T), extraterritorial organisations (U). Education (P85) and health (Q): only their capitalist part is productive. The public and non-profit part is estimated by country (see ‘Public and non-profit education and health’ below).
- **N** = Σ<sub>productive</sub> employees (EMPE) · **H** = Σ<sub>productive</sub> hours of employees (H\_EMPE) · **v** = Σ<sub>productive</sub> compensation (COMP).
- **pv** = Σ<sub>market</sub> VA − Σ<sub>market</sub> (LAB − COMP) − v. LAB − COMP is the labour income imputed to own-account (self-employed) workers, who are not wage earners of capital.

<div class="f">e = pv / v · surplus labour = H · e / (1 + e) · necessary labour = H / (1 + e) · world e in hours = Σ surplus labour / Σ necessary labour</div>

**China.** WIOD does not distinguish employees in China (COMP = LAB). With the ILO (modelled estimates, `EMP_2EMP_SEX_STE_NB`), the share of employees among non-agricultural persons employed is calculated:

<div class="f">s<sub>t</sub> = ILO employees<sub>t</sub> / [ILO employed<sub>t</sub> × (1 − WIOD agricultural share<sub>t</sub>)], capped at 1</div>

In non-agricultural industries, compensation and employment are multiplied by s; in agriculture (A01–A03), almost entirely family-based, s = 0 and all its compensation is treated as own-account income. Hours: 46 h a week × 50 weeks per employee. The previous method, which also applied the share of employees in the whole economy to agriculture, is kept in the sensitivity table (+3%).

**(b) 2015–last year, OECD countries: national accounts by industry** (OECD tables 6 and 7, SNA 2008: `DSD_NAMAIN10@DF_TABLE6` and `@DF_TABLE7`). Same criterion at the level of ISIC Rev. 4 sections: unproductive G, K, M69–M70, M73; outside L, O, T, U (and P, Q in the second variant).

<div class="f">v = Σ<sub>prod</sub> D1 · cp = Σ<sub>market</sub> SELF<sub>persons</sub> × D1/SAL<sub>persons</sub> · pv = Σ<sub>market</sub> B1G − cp − v · N = Σ<sub>prod</sub> SAL<sub>persons</sub> · H = Σ<sub>prod</sub> SAL<sub>hours</sub> · r = Σ<sub>market</sub> P51C / v</div>

The OECD series are chained to the WIOD level of 2014: X<sub>t</sub> = X<sub>WIOD,2014</sub> × X<sub>OECD,t</sub> / X<sub>OECD,2014</sub>, for X = e, N, hours per employee and r. WIOD thus sets the level and the OECD contributes the change measured by industry. Completeness rules: missing T and U are set to zero; if sections that are all productive and market sections are missing (Japan: E, N, S), their sum is obtained as a residual from the total; if a country does not report M69–M70 and M73, their weight in M is taken from WIOD 2014; a section with no data on non-employees is set to zero; e is chained only as long as the imputation for non-employees is the same as in 2014. Korea is left out (it lacks unproductive sections). Mexico, Turkey, Cyprus and Malta do not publish employees by industry: their e comes from the OECD and their N from the ILO. After each country's last OECD year, the series continues with the ILO change. The OECD/WIOD ratios for 2014 are published as a diagnostic (`mundo_horas_ocde_diagnostico.csv`): in most countries they lie between 0.95 and 1.05 for N and for H.

**(b′) From 2014, countries without employees by industry in the OECD: ILO surveys by section.** For Brazil, Indonesia, India, Russia, Mexico, Turkey, Cyprus and Malta, the ILOSTAT series by ISIC Rev. 4 section are used: employees (`EES_TEES_SEX_ECO_NB`), actual weekly hours (`HOW_TEMP_SEX_ECO_NB`) and average monthly earnings in national currency (`EAR_EMTA_SEX_ECO_CUR_NB`), always from a single survey per country (the one covering most years from 2014). With the same industry criterion, and with the weight of M69–M70 and M73 within M taken from WIOD 2014:

<div class="f">ps = productive employees / employees · hp = average hours in productive sections · q = productive wage bill / wage bill</div>

From the first survey year b (2014 in most cases; India 2018; Russia 2017), the result of the aggregate extension is corrected as follows:

<div class="f">N<sub>t</sub> = N<sup>aggregate</sup><sub>t</sub> × ps<sub>t</sub>/ps<sub>b</sub> · h<sub>t</sub> = h<sub>b</sub> × hp<sub>t</sub>/hp<sub>b</sub> · 1 + e<sub>t</sub> = (1 + e<sup>aggregate</sup><sub>t</sub>) × q<sub>b</sub>/q<sub>t</sub></div>

In Mexico and Turkey, e comes from the OECD accounts by industry and only N and h are corrected. After the last survey year the proportions are held constant. China does not publish employees by section in ILOSTAT (its labour force survey has been released only in aggregate form since 2016), so its number of employees remains in the aggregate extension (its rate of surplus value is measured by industry with TiM up to 2022; see b‴). The diagnostic by country is in `mundo_horas_ilo_secciones.csv`. Limitations: India's employee series in the ILO covers only regular workers, so its productive share is measured among them; the sections do not separate M69–M70 and M73.

**(b″) From 2014, surplus by industry outside the OECD: UN national accounts (table 2.6).** Table 2.6 of the UN database of official national accounts (UNdata, SNA) publishes, by ISIC Rev. 4 section, value added, compensation of employees, mixed income, operating surplus and consumption of fixed capital. With it, for Brazil and India, the rate of surplus value no longer follows the aggregate labour share and is instead measured industry by industry with the same criterion as in WIOD and the OECD:

<div class="f">v = Σ<sub>productive</sub> compensation · pv = VA<sub>market</sub> − mixed income<sub>market</sub> − v · e = pv / v · r = CCF<sub>market</sub> / v</div>

Each series published by the country (a UN ‘series’ is a segment with a homogeneous base and method) is chained separately: its first year from 2014 is set equal to the value of the previous trajectory and from there its change governs; after its last year the previous change continues. Up to four missing sections are tolerated (set to zero) when they are not heavily weighted productive sections. Result: Brazil measured in 2015–2020 (level of 1 + e in 2014, UN versus WIOD: 1.08; change in e between 2014 and 2020: +10%) and India in 2015–2020 (0.72; −11%). The level differences do not affect the series, which takes only the change from the UN. The diagnostics are in `mundo_horas_onu_ramas.csv` and `mundo_horas_onu_diagnostico.csv`. China and Indonesia do not have table 2.6 in the UN database, and Russia's ends in 2013. Indonesia and Russia remain with the previous measurement; China is measured with the OECD (b‴). In the years measured by the UN, the ILO q correction is not applied to e; after the last measured year, the correction is re-anchored in that year (q<sub>last UN</sub>/q<sub>t</sub>). It is applied to N and to hours. In India, r follows in 2015–2020 the change in the UN's consumption of fixed capital by industry, chained to the calibrated r of 2014.

**(b‴) China from 2014: employment and labour income by industry from the OECD (TiM).** The OECD's Trade in Employment database (2025 edition, consistent with the ICIO world tables) publishes, by industry and up to 2022, persons employed, labour compensation and its share of value added. For China, that compensation is total labour income, including that of own-account workers (in agriculture it exceeds value added), as in WIOD. The same method as with WIOD is applied: s<sub>t</sub> = employees<sub>ILO</sub> / (employment<sub>ILO</sub> × (1 − agricultural share of TiM employment)), with no employees in family agriculture; v = Σ<sub>productive</sub> labour income × s; cp = Σ<sub>market</sub> labour income × (1 − s) (that of non-employees); pv = Σ<sub>market</sub> value added − cp − v; e = pv / v. As in WIOD, the wages of the circulation industries remain within pv. The part of M corresponding to legal and accounting services and advertising is taken from WIOD 2014. The series is chained to the WIOD level of 2014 like the UN series. In 2014 the TiM level is somewhat higher than the WIOD level (1 + e: 3.06 versus 2.77, with education and health productive), a difference of base and classification that the splice neutralises, because it takes only the change from TiM. Between 2014 and 2022 the rate of surplus value measured in this way moves little (from 1.98 to 2.02 in the central variant), somewhat below the rate given by the ILO labour share in 2018 (1.96 versus 2.01). There is no consumption of fixed capital by industry: depreciation follows the aggregate extension. That method assumes that TiM compensation includes the income of non-employees, which is only the case for China. Results in `mundo_horas_tim_ramas.csv` and `mundo_horas_tim_diagnostico.csv`.

**(b⁗) Indonesia and Russia from 2014; India as a sensitivity: TiM with the income of non-employees imputed.** In these countries, TiM compensation is that of employees only: it does not include mixed income (in Indian agriculture it is 14–17% of value added, against 100% in China). The same rule is applied as for OECD countries: the labour income of the non-employees of each section is imputed with the average wage of that section's employees, cp<sub>j</sub> = compensation<sub>j</sub> × (1 − s<sub>j</sub>) / s<sub>j</sub>, where s<sub>j</sub> is the share of employees among those employed in the section in the ILO labour force surveys, capped at the section's value added; v = Σ<sub>productive</sub> compensation; pv = Σ<sub>market</sub> value added − cp − v. Years without a survey take an interpolated s<sub>j</sub> (Indonesia, 2016–2022). The series is chained to the WIOD level of 2014 and contributes only its change. In Russia, the TiM level almost coincides with WIOD's (e of 1.44 against 1.47 in 2014) and the rate of surplus value measured by industry stays almost constant up to 2022; in Indonesia (1.53 against 2.39) it falls by 11% between 2014 and 2022, whereas the aggregate extension made it rise. In India, the imputation takes e to 0.79, very far from the UN and WIOD (≈ 3), because the income of the Indian self-employed is well below the average wage of employees; moreover, the first year that can be chained is 2020, the year of the pandemic. India therefore remains in the central estimate with the UN up to 2020 and the aggregate extension thereafter, and the TiM variant (e × 1.16 from 2022) appears in the sensitivity table: it adds 1.2% to the world mass in 2025 (author's decision, 28 November 2026).

**(c) 2015–2025, aggregate extension with the ILO** (the basis for all countries outside the OECD; in China, for the number of employees from 2014 and for e from 2023).

<div class="f">e<sub>t</sub> = e<sub>2014</sub> × [(1 − LS<sub>t</sub>)/LS<sub>t</sub>] / [(1 − LS<sub>2014</sub>)/LS<sub>2014</sub>] · N<sub>t</sub> = N<sub>2014</sub> × A<sub>t</sub>/A<sub>2014</sub> · h<sub>t</sub> = h<sub>2014</sub> × hs<sub>t</sub>/hs<sub>2014</sub></div>

- **LS**: labour share of income (ILO, SDG 10.4.1, `LAP_2GDP_NOC_RT`).
- **A**: total employees, modelled and harmonised ILO estimates (`EMP_2EMP_SEX_STE_NB`); the 2014 productive share is held constant. The series by section (`EES_TEES_SEX_ECO_NB`) have survey breaks and are not used.
- **India**: WIOD counts as employees both regular workers and casual labourers, as the NSS surveys do; the ILO employee series covers only regular workers (91 million in 2014, against 315 million in WIOD) and grows by 64% up to 2025. Therefore, for India, A<sub>t</sub> = ILO employed<sub>t</sub> × (1 − own-account share<sub>t</sub>), with the own-account share in usual status from NSS 2011-12 (52.2%) and PLFS 2017-18 (52.2%), 2021-22 (55.8%), 2022-23 (57.3%) and 2023-24 (58.4%), interpolated and held constant after the last data point. Result: +13% employees between 2014 and 2025, instead of +64%.
- **hs**: weekly hours per person employed (`HOW_TEMP_SEX_ECO_NB`); if missing, constant.

**Version net of depreciation.** e<sub>net</sub> = e − r, with r = consumption of fixed capital of market industries / v. In OECD countries, r is measured by industry (P51C) from 2014. In the other years and countries:

<div class="f">r = k · (δ·K/Y)<sub>PWT</sub> × VA<sub>market</sub> / v · with VA<sub>market</sub>/v = 1 + e + cp/v in the chained years</div>

(δ·K/Y)<sub>PWT</sub> = delta · cn / cgdpo from the Penn World Table (last value held constant after 2023). Since that depreciation covers all fixed capital, including dwellings and public capital, it is calibrated with k = r<sub>OECD,2014</sub> / r<sub>PWT,2014</sub> of the country itself or, outside the OECD, with the median of those ratios (0.76). The calibration table is in `mundo_horas_calibracion_depreciacion.csv`. In the net version, the fall in e after 2008 mainly reflects the rise in depreciation measured by the PWT in China and India; it is an estimate, not a measurement by industry.

**Sensitivity** (`mundo_horas_sensibilidad.csv`). Each row changes a single assumption: net version; Chinese working day of 44 or 49 h; India with the ILO employee level (regular workers only); agriculture in India and China outside capitalist production; previous method for China. **Criterion adopted** (theoretical decision of the author): casual labourers are productive workers whenever their surplus labour is converted into surplus value, whatever the duration of their employment; agricultural labour is productive whenever the wages of those workers are money invested as capital, and not money spent as a mere means of circulation to buy unproductive labour. That is why the central estimate includes them. The rows ‘India: ILO employee level’ and ‘agriculture outside’ are not alternative interpretations of the criterion: they show how much those workers weigh in the mass (between 12% and 23%). The practical limit of the criterion is statistical: the accounts do not allow casual labourers hired by capitalist enterprises to be separated from those hired by households for their own consumption; the latter are excluded only when they appear in the industry of households as employers (T).

Other caveats: there is no within-industry correction; because of differences in definition, the US e in this series does not coincide with that of the United States section; observations with e outside (0, 8) in the WIOD years are excluded and recorded in `mundo_horas_excluidos.csv` (the filter is not applied to the extension); after adjustments B3 and B4, e has a floor of 0.01, and in B4 a labour share of 0.55 is assumed where the ILO does not publish it. The source of e, N and r for each country and year is in `mundo_horas_paises.csv`.

**Public and non-profit education and health.** The full calculation is carried out with education and health inside (‘productive’ variant) and outside (‘outside’ variant). The central estimate combines both with the non-capitalist part g of each country and year:

<div class="f">g = (employed S13 + employed S15 − employed O) / employed (P + Q), between 0 and 1</div>

S13 is general government and S15 non-profit institutions (OECD, employment by institutional sector, `DSD_NAMAIN10@DF_TABLE3_EMP_SECTOR`); O, P and Q come from table 7. Outside those accounts, the share of public employment in P + Q from ILO surveys (`EMP_PUBL_SEX_ECO_NB`) is used when it is plausible (between 0.2 and 1), and failing that, the median of the measured countries. In the United States, the non-profit part from the BLS is added to the public part. Since N, H, necessary hours and surplus-labour hours are sums by section, the linear combination (1 − g) · inside + g · outside approximates the measurement with only the capitalist part of education and health. It does not reproduce it exactly, because e is a ratio for each country: recalculated directly for 2014, surplus labour would be between 0.25% and 0.6% greater in the United States, the United Kingdom, France and Germany. Limits: S15 also includes religious and civil organisations, so g tends to be overestimated; the ILO public employment surveys are heterogeneous; China, Russia, Canada, Australia, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden and Cyprus use the median. Details in `mundo_horas_parte_publica.csv`.

**State enterprises.** Employees of state enterprises that sell commodities for profit count as productive (author's decision): their wages are capital advanced by the state. They are not separated, because the accounts by industry include them together with those of private enterprises.
