## Interpretative framework

**Project.** The cognitive map seeks to take up and continue the necessarily unfinished project of the Marxian critique of political economy, not to do critical political economy: the categories (value, surplus value, capital, productive labour) are forms of a historically specific social relation, and they decide what is measured before anything is measured. The series record the phenomenal manifestation of these determinations; they do not demonstrate them (see the [Reading](lectura.html#critica)). Artificial intelligence is used to gather, harmonise and recalculate a quantity of official data that could not be processed by hand, in the same way that Marx, in his lifetime, processed an enormous body of empirical material in order to elaborate the concept of capital; it makes no theoretical decision.

**Aim.** The cognitive map seeks to account for the fundamental tendencies of contemporary capitalist production. To that end it combines three operations, each documented on this page:

1. **Translating empirical data into categories.** National accounts, employment, hours, capital stocks, trade and input–output tables are converted into the rate and mass of surplus value, the composition of capital, necessary labour time and surplus labour, with explicit theoretical decisions (§3 to §13).
2. **Cross-checking interpretations.** Six currents of the Marxist debate read the same data, in order to see what withstands every reading and what depends on each one (§6). The critical reading of these currents is made from the framework of the doctoral research.
3. **Recording the state of the productive forces.** The time freed by the relative reduction of necessary labour, and what capital turns it into, is measured alongside profit: it is the material basis of the possibility of disposable time (§9).

The data are interpreted from the standpoint of the Marxian critique of political economy. Profit is a form of appearance of surplus value; surplus value is the unpaid labour of productively exploited workers; the rate of profit is the ratio between surplus value and the total capital advanced, that is, the sum of constant capital and variable capital: pv/(c + v). The mass of surplus value is the product of the rate of surplus value (pv/v, the degree of exploitation) and the number of productively exploited workers.

Official statistics are not constructed with these categories, so translating them requires theoretical decisions. The site does not hide these decisions in a single number: it turns them into *forks* of a multiverse and shows which results withstand all of them and which depend on the theory adopted.

The series show the phenomenal manifestation of categorial determinations; they do not demonstrate them. Nor do they measure capital's ‘distance’ from a ceiling: what they allow us to observe is the concrete way in which the dynamic of the rate and mass of surplus value, and the field of its countertendencies, unfolds.

The theoretical interpretation comes from the doctoral research of Pablo Ignacio Jiménez Cea, *Los límites inmanentes del capital.
