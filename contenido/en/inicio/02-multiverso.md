## United States: the spread of the multiverse

{{m-n}} specifications of the US rate of profit, each normalised to its period average (= 100). The light band covers the 10th to 90th percentiles; the dark band, the 25th to 75th; the line is the median.

There is no single way of measuring the rate of profit: it depends on what is counted as profit and what as capital, and each theoretical current decides differently. Instead of choosing one, the cognitive map calculates all the reasonable combinations and draws them together. The line is the typical value; the bands show how far the measurements diverge from one another. If the band is narrow, the theories agree; if it is wide, the result depends on the theory. What to look at is the shape: the rate falls from the post-war period until the mid-1970s, oscillates at a low level until the early 2000s and recovers afterwards; at no point does it describe a continuous fall towards zero.

:::como How is this chart calculated?
Each specification is an annual rate r = numerator / denominator. The numerator changes according to the fork: net surplus, profit before or after tax, or broad surplus value by industry. The denominator is the BEA's year-end net stock of fixed assets (K), at current or historical cost, with or without intellectual property products and inventories, and either all capital or only that of the productive industries (in that case, the depreciation of the unproductive industries is not subtracted from the numerator). In the business sector there is also a fork with variable capital in the denominator: pv/(K + V). The full list is in the [methodology, §5](metodologia.html#multiverso).

To compare specifications at different levels, each series is divided by its period average and multiplied by 100. Then, for each year, the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 90th percentiles of all the series are calculated. The fan is not a confidence interval: it shows how much the level of the rate depends on theoretical decisions.
:::
