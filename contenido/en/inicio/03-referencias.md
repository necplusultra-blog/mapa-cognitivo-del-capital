## Six readings in the debate faced with the same data

The Marxist debate on the rate of profit is not only about figures: it is about what profit is, what capital is and what it means for the rate to fall or rise. Here six currents are set to read the same data for the United States and the world. Each line in the chart is the rate calculated in the manner of one of them; each card, below, summarises what that current looks at, what it sees in these data and what diagnosis it draws. Hover over a card (or tap it) to highlight its line. The lines do not coincide in level, because they count different things, but they do coincide in much of their movement: all of them fall until the early 1980s and none, in the broad measures of surplus value, returns to its post-war level. The critical reading of the six, from the framework of this research, is in the [Reading](lectura.html#seis-lecturas).

:::lectura emp_ramas_cc | Shaikh (and Tonak): the law operates through real competition
**What it looks at.** Surplus value in the broad sense: all the new value of the productive industries minus the wages of their workers, over fixed capital. It distinguishes productive and unproductive labour (Shaikh and Tonak, 1994).

**What it sees here.** The rate falls from {{lx-ra-48}}% in 1948 to {{lx-ra-82}}% in 1982 and recovers to {{lx-ra-u}}, without returning to its post-war level. The recovery rests on the rate of surplus value, which rises from {{lx-e72}} to {{lx-eu}} with the within-industry correction: wages grow more slowly than productivity.

**Diagnosis.** A long-run falling tendency that operates through competition between capitals and manifests itself in long waves. The neoliberal phase was a recovery of the profit of enterprise sustained by wage restraint and the fall in the rate of interest, and the 2008 crisis marked its exhaustion (Shaikh, 2016).
:::

:::lectura emp_intra_cc | Moseley: the surplus value absorbed by unproductive labour
**What it looks at.** The rate of surplus value of production and non-supervisory workers in the productive industries, and the part of surplus value spent on circulation and supervisory labour (Moseley, 1991).

**What it sees here.** The surplus value produced and the profit appropriated diverge. The within-industry rate goes from {{lx-in-82}}% in 1982 to {{lx-in-max}} and stands at {{lx-in-u}}%, above its 1974–1979 levels, while the Duménil-Lévy-type corporate rate remains below that of the 1960s.

**Diagnosis.** The post-war fall in the conventional rate was due above all to the growth of unproductive labour, which absorbs surplus value without producing it, rather than to the composition of capital. The subsequent recovery stems from a rising rate of surplus value and a relative curbing of that expenditure.
:::

:::lectura corp_ebe_hc | Kliman: a rate that never recovered
**What it looks at.** Corporate profit over capital valued at what it actually cost (historical cost), in the temporal single-system interpretation (TSSI).

**What it sees here.** The rate falls from {{lx-kl-max}} and, since the 1980s, has moved well below its 1950s level: {{lx-kl-19}}% in 2019. The jump to {{lx-kl-u}}% after 2021 coincides with inflation, which inflates current profits relative to a capital valued at past prices.

**Diagnosis.** The crises of the 1970s did not destroy enough capital value to restore profitability. Without that recovery, accumulation weakened, debt grew in order to sustain it, and the 2008 crisis was its outcome (Kliman, 2012).
:::

:::lectura nfc_ebe_cc | Duménil and Lévy: neoliberalism as restoration
**What it looks at.** The net surplus of non-financial corporations over their fixed capital at current cost.

**What it sees here.** The rate falls from {{lx-dl-65}}% in 1965 to {{lx-dl-82}}% in 1982 and recovers to {{lx-dl-u}} (up {{lx-dl-rec}} from the trough), without returning to the level of the 1960s.

**Diagnosis.** The profitability crisis of the 1970s was resolved by neoliberalism, a social order that restored the income and power of the capitalist classes, in alliance with the managerial cadres. The 2008 crisis was a crisis of that order —financialisation, indebtedness, US imbalances—, not of a new fall in the rate (Duménil and Lévy, 2004 and 2011).
:::

:::lectura emp_ramas_cv | Roberts and Carchedi: the law as the ultimate cause of crises
**What it looks at.** Surplus value over fixed capital plus variable capital, pv/(c + v), and the world rate of profit.

**What it sees here.** The US rate falls from {{lx-cv-48}}% in 1948 to {{lx-cv-u}}. The net world rate (Penn World Table) goes from {{lx-w-07}}% in 2007 to {{lx-w-u}}. The K/V composition rises again after 2000 ({{lx-kv00}} → {{lx-kvu}}), and in the phase beginning in 2004 the rate falls in all the business-sector specifications.

**Diagnosis.** The rise in the organic composition pushes the rate down in the long run; the countertendencies only check it for a time. Crises break out when the mass of profit falls, and since 2008 the world economy has been living through a ‘long depression’ (Roberts, 2016; Carchedi and Roberts, 2022).
:::

:::lectura | Heinrich: there is no law that the data could confirm
**What it looks at.** It does not propose a measure. It reads the entire spread of the multiverse and asks whether the fall can be deduced from the determinations of capital.

**What it sees here.** Only {{lx-dec-rob}} of the {{lx-dec-n}} changes between decades hold across all specifications. The K/V composition rose until 1982 ({{lx-kv82}}), fell until 2000 owing to the cheapening of capital, and rose again. The level and the direction depend on the theory adopted.

**Diagnosis.** The law cannot be demonstrated in general: the same increase in productive force that raises the technical composition cheapens constant capital and raises the rate of surplus value, and the net effect remains indeterminate. Crises must be explained by credit, money and overaccumulation, not by a tendency of the rate (Heinrich, 2013).
:::

:::como How are the lines and the table calculated?
- **Duménil-Lévy type:** net operating surplus of non-financial corporations (NIPA 1.14) / their net stock of fixed assets at current cost.
- **Kliman type:** net surplus of corporations (total) / net stock at historical cost.
- **Shaikh-Tonak type (broad surplus value by industry):** (business value added − rental housing − financial services paid for by households out of their wages − depreciation − compensation in the productive industries) / net non-residential stock of the sector. Trade, finance and insurance, real estate and legal services are excluded from the productive industries.
- **Moseley type (broad surplus value within-industry,** from 1972): the same, but only the compensation of production and non-supervisory workers in those industries is subtracted.
- **Roberts-Carchedi type, pv/(c + v):** surplus value by industry divided by fixed capital plus annual variable capital. Since v is taken as an annual flow, it assumes a single turnover per year. The world rate is the Penn World Table net rate, weighted by capital, including China.
- **Business sector, all productive:** like the Shaikh-Tonak rate, but subtracting all the compensation of the sector. It is the baseline without the distinction between productive and unproductive labour.
- **Table:** value of each rate in the years indicated; the last column is the percentage change between 1982 (the trough of the broad business-sector measures; in the Duménil-Lévy and Kliman types the trough is 2001) and the latest year.

All include intellectual property products in K, without inventories or adjustment for utilisation. They are definitions ‘in the manner of’ the authors, not replications of their series: the series reconstructed with each author's definition are in the [next section](#replicacion). The diagnoses summarise the published theses of each current; they are not conclusions of the seismograph. Full references in the [methodology, §6](metodologia.html#referencias).
:::
