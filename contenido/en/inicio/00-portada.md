# Seismograph of Capital

The aim of the seismograph is to give an account of the fundamental tendencies of contemporary capitalist production. It does so along two paths that intersect: the study of a wide variety of empirical data —national accounts, employment, hours of labour, capital, trade, input–output tables—, translated into the categories of the critique of political economy, and the confrontation of the main Marxist interpretations, which are set to read the same data. A seismograph does not predict the earthquake: it records tremors and ruptures.
{: .lede }

It records the rate and the mass of profit and of surplus value as a *multiverse* of specifications, each tied to an explicit theoretical decision, and it is updated when the sources publish new data. It also records the state of the productive forces that capital develops: how much labour time is needed to reproduce those who produce, and what capital turns the time freed by that development into. Contained in that conversion, as an objective possibility, is [disposable time](#fuerzas).

The seismograph seeks to take up and continue the necessarily unfinished project of the [Marxian critique of political economy](lectura.html#critica), not to do critical political economy. That is why the qualitative comes first: profit is a form of appearance of surplus value, surplus value is the unpaid labour of productively exploited workers, and the figures record the manifestation of these conceptual determinations; they do not prove them. Just as Marx, in his lifetime, processed an enormous amount of economic data in order to elaborate the concept of capital, the seismograph uses artificial intelligence to process data and official sources that today would be impossible to gather by hand; the theoretical decisions that guide this processing are explicit and are documented at every fork. The [full methodology](metodologia.html) brings together sources, formulas, decisions, splices and limitations, so that any researcher can audit the results. [A first reading of the results](lectura.html) interprets them from the standpoint of the theory of the immanent limits of capital.

The seismograph is a piece of work carried out within the framework of my doctoral thesis project, *Los límites inmanentes del capital. Crisis, contratendencias y la hipótesis del capitalismo limítrofe* (Doctoral Programme in Sociology, ICSyH ‘Alfonso Vélez Pliego’, BUAP), which I am pursuing with a scholarship from Mexico’s Secretariat of Science, Humanities, Technology and Innovation (SECIHTI), number 4052073. I thank the Mexican working class, whose labour makes this scholarship possible, and I hope I am putting it to good use. — Pablo Ignacio Jiménez Cea
