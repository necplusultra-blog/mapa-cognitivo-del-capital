# Cognitive Map of Contemporary Capitalism

The aim of this cognitive map of capitalism is to give an account of the fundamental tendencies of contemporary capitalism. It does so along two paths that converge and intersect: the study of a wide variety of empirical data —national statistics on employment, hours of labour, capital, trade and input–output tables, as well as official statistics on the same matters from international bodies such as the OECD, the UN and the ILO—, translated into the categories of the critique of political economy, and the confrontation of the main contemporary Marxist readings that investigate those same data.

The theoretical foundation of this project lies, consequently, in Marx's critique of political economy —unfolded by Marx in writings such as *Capital* and the *Grundrisse*, as well as in the various manuscripts linked to his project of an immanent critique of capitalist society— and in a critical appropriation of its various contemporary readings —such as, for example, those of Moishe Postone, Michael Heinrich, Robert Kurz and Michael Roberts, among others—.

This project is called *Cognitive Map of Capital* because it is a critical cartography of capitalist social relations on a global scale. What is at stake here is to record the empirical manifestations of the fundamental tendencies of the capitalist mode of production —its crises included—, with the aim of contributing a critical reading of contemporary capitalism and of bringing to light the possibilities of determinate negation that are developing within it today.

As Fredric Jameson (1988, p. 353) argued, the elaboration of a cognitive map of our historical moment, which implies a cognition and a critical representation of capitalist relations on a planetary scale, is an integral part of any political project oriented towards the emancipatory transformation of existing society. That is precisely the task to which this cognitive map is devoted.
{: .lede }

This project records the rate and the mass of profit and of surplus value as a complex *multiverse* of specifications, linking the study of each statistical variable analysed to an explicit theoretical decision, and it is updated whenever the sources publish new data. It also records the state of the productive forces that capital develops: how much labour time is needed to reproduce those who produce, and what capital turns the time freed by that development into. Contained in that conversion, as an objective possibility, is [disposable time](#fuerzas).

With this cognitive map, as with my research, I seek to take up and continue the necessarily unfinished project of [Marx's critique of political economy](reading.html#critica), not to do critical political economy. As Dr Alfonso García Vela pointed out to me, in a project of this kind it is important to avoid relapsing into a mere Marxist economics, into a critical political economy, since, although the quantitative question carries enormous weight in capitalist society, what is fundamental in it is the category of capital —the logic of its concept, which determines its real movement—. That is why, in this project, the immanent critique of capitalist society comes first: profit is set out as a form of appearance of surplus value, surplus value is the unpaid labour of the productively exploited fraction of the working class, and the figures record the empirical manifestation —on the surface of society— of those deeper conceptual determinations; they do not demonstrate them.

Just as Marx, in his lifetime, processed an enormous quantity of economic data available in the British Museum in order to elaborate the concept of capital, the cognitive map uses artificial intelligence to process economic data and official statistical sources that today would be impossible for a single person —or even for a group of people devoted to the task— to gather by hand. It would be absurd, and counterproductive, for an immanent critique of established society to refuse to use the most advanced technological tools of our epoch, even while holding a critical perspective on the determinations of those tools as capital —that is, as technologies that in no way exist neutrally, but are constituted by capital—. The theoretical decisions that guide the analytical processing done by the machine are, however, explicit and documented at each fork. The [full methodology](methodology.html) brings together sources, formulae, decisions, splices and limitations, so that any researcher can audit the results. The [first reading of the results](reading.html) interprets them from the standpoint of the Marxian theory of the immanent limits of capital that is the object of my research.

This cognitive map of contemporary capitalism is, in this sense, a project carried out within the broader framework of my doctoral thesis project, *Los límites inmanentes del capital. Crisis, contratendencias y la hipótesis del capitalismo limítrofe* (Doctorate in Sociology, ICSyH 'Alfonso Vélez Pliego', BUAP), supervised by Dr Alfonso García Vela, which I am developing thanks to a scholarship granted by Mexico's Secretariat of Science, Humanities, Technology and Innovation (SECIHTI), number 4052073. I am grateful to the Mexican working class, whose labour makes this scholarship possible, and I hope to be putting it to good use by producing knowledge oriented towards the radical transformation of society. — Pablo Ignacio Jiménez Cea
