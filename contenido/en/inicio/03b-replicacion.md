## The authors' series, reconstructed with today's data

The lines in the previous section follow the spirit of each current with a common definition. Here something else is done: the series that each author published is reconstructed, with the author's own definition but with the current national accounts, and compared with the values the author gave. The accounts have changed a great deal since then (intellectual property products began to be counted as capital in 1999 and 2013, and compensation and profits have been revised several times), so the point is not to hit their numbers but to find out whether the tendencies that each reading interprets are still there. The chart puts the five series on a common scale (1982 = 100), because their levels cannot be compared: each counts different things. The table states, point by point, what is reproduced and what is not.

**What holds.** Roberts's ‘whole economy’ measure falls {{rp-ro-6582}} between 1965 and 1982 at historical cost ({{rp-ro-cc-6582}} at current cost) and rises {{rp-ro-8297}} between 1982 and 1997 ({{rp-ro-cc-8297}}), almost the same as what he published in 2018. Shaikh and Tonak's rate of surplus value, with the wages of production workers as variable capital, is reproduced in the years that can be compared (1975–1982, with differences of a few hundredths) and keeps rising: from {{rp-st-e82}} in 1982 to {{rp-st-eu}} in {{rp-fin}}. Kliman's before-tax rate at historical cost gives {{rp-kl78}} in 1978 and {{rp-kl01}} in 2001, against the 24% and 12% he reports. Moseley's proportion of unproductive labour (Uf/V) in 1977 is {{rp-uf77}}, the same as he estimated.

**What changes.** The recovery that Duménil and Lévy saw between 1982 and 2000 was some 45%; with today's data it is {{rp-dl-rec}}. The fall from 1965 to 1982 ({{rp-dl-6582}}) is still there, but the thesis of neoliberalism as a restoration of profitability rests, in the current measurement, on a weaker recovery up to 2000, which continues afterwards. Kliman's thesis, by contrast, depends on the measure chosen: between 1982 and 1997 property income over capital at historical cost changes by {{rp-kl-pi}} and total profit by {{rp-kl-tp}} (there is no recovery), but before-tax profit rises {{rp-kl-pat}}. Moseley's conventional rate in 1977 gives {{rp-mo77}}, against the 12% he published, with surplus value net of the business sector's taxes on production (net of subsidies) and capital including inventories; this is our reading of his identity, not a definition he wrote in those words. The remaining gap, like that of his rate of surplus value and that of his unproductive capital, reflects at least in part the revisions to the accounts since 1991.

:::como How is each series reconstructed?
- **Shaikh and Tonak (1994, tables 5.6 and 5.8).** S\* = net product of the business sector (excluding rental housing) minus V\*; V\* = compensation of production and non-supervisory workers in the productive industries (the seismograph's within-industry correction, from 1972). What households pay to finance is not subtracted, because they do not subtract it. The Marxian rate is S\*/K, with K the net stock at current cost; they use the gross stock, which is no longer published, so only the trend is comparable. From 1948 there is a version with V\* equal to all the compensation in the productive industries, which is a lower bound of their rate of surplus value.
- **Moseley (1991, ch. 3; 1977 figures in Moseley, 1997).** V = production and non-supervisory wages in the productive industries; Uf = the rest of the business sector's compensation (circulation and supervision); C = fixed capital of the productive industries plus inventories; Us = fixed capital of trade, finance and insurance, non-residential real estate, rental and leasing, and legal services. Conventional rate = (S − Uf)/(C + Us), with S net of the business sector's taxes on production net of subsidies (NIPA 1.13, lines 9 and 17; see above).
- **Kliman (2010; 2012, ch. 5).** Corporations (NIPA 1.14). Property income = net value added − compensation; total profit = net operating surplus; before-tax profit = profits with inventory valuation adjustment. In all three, depreciation at current cost is replaced by depreciation at historical cost, and the denominator is the net stock at historical cost at the end of the previous year (the capital advanced).
- **Duménil and Lévy (2002, table 3).** Broad rate r_b = (net value added of corporations − compensation) / net non-residential stock at current cost. The ratio between years is compared, because the article does not publish annual levels. There is a variant without intellectual property products (neither their investment in the numerator nor their stock in the denominator), closer to the accounts they used.
- **Roberts (2018; 2021).** Surplus value = national income − compensation of employees; C = private non-residential fixed assets, at historical cost and at current cost; v = compensation; rate = s/(C + v).
- **Result.** For levels, ‘reproduces’ if the difference is up to 10% of the published value and ‘approximates’ if it is up to 25%. For changes, ‘reproduces’ if the difference is up to 5 points and the direction coincides, and ‘approximates’ if it is up to 12 points. Beyond that, it is indicated whether at least the direction coincides. The level of Shaikh and Tonak's Marxian rate is marked as not comparable, because they use the gross stock and the net stock is used here; for that rate the comparison of changes is what counts.

The BEA accounts are revised every year, and comprehensively every five. That is why an author's figures from 1994 or 2002 cannot coincide exactly with today's, even if the definition is the same. Details of the definitions and sources in the [methodology](metodologia.html#replicacion).
:::
