## The artificial intelligence corporations

Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Nvidia, Oracle, Broadcom and AMD, according to their financial statements filed with the SEC. In {{ia-fin}} they appropriated {{ia-pct-corp}} of US corporate profits and around {{ia-pct-mundo}} of the world mass of gross profit; their investment is equivalent to {{ia-pct-inv}} of US private non-residential investment.

### Rate of profit: AI group versus the non-financial sector

The profit of eight large artificial intelligence companies per dollar of fixed capital, compared with that of non-financial companies as a whole. They earn much more per dollar invested, not because their workers produce more surplus value, but because they control a condition that all capital needs and appropriate surplus value produced elsewhere.

:::como How are these charts calculated?
- **AI group rate:** Σ operating profit / Σ total assets of the eight companies (SEC XBRL: OperatingIncomeLoss, Assets; calendar years).
- **US non-financial:** net operating surplus of non-financial corporations / their net stock at historical cost without intellectual property products, because business accounting does not capitalise in-house R&D.
- **Without the AI group:** (non-financial profit − operating profit of the group) / (non-financial K − net property, plant and equipment of the group).
- **Weights:** pre-tax profit of the group / US corporate profits (NIPA); investment in fixed assets of the group / private non-residential investment; pre-tax profit / PWT world mass of gross profit converted to current dollars.
- **Table:** operating profit / net property, plant and equipment of each company, in %.
:::

Caveats: (1) the profits of these companies are consolidated on a world scale; the US non-financial aggregate is domestic, so the ‘without AI’ rate is approximate. (2) Capital is book capital, at historical cost, and in-house R&D is not capitalised. (3) Appropriated profit is not produced surplus value: these shares measure appropriation, which includes rents and transfers, not the formation of surplus value. (4) The world mass for the years without Penn World Table data uses the last available wage share.
