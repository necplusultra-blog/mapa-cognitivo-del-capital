## The field of countertendencies

Marx (2021, pp. 297–307) sets out six causes that counteract the tendential fall of the rate of profit: the raising of the degree of exploitation, the reduction of wages below their value, the cheapening of the elements of constant capital, relative surplus population, foreign trade and the increase in share capital. They are not external forces that correct the tendency from outside: they are produced by the same causes that produce the fall. This section brings together observable indicators for each of them, and begins with the central question: in each phase, what sustained or sank the rate of profit?

### What moved the rate in each phase

The rate of profit can be written as r = e · v/K: it rises if the rate of surplus value e rises and falls if the composition K/V rises. The phases are not chosen in advance: their boundaries are the most frequent break years in the multiverse ({{ct-cortes}}). Each bar measures, in logarithms, how much each factor contributed to the change in the rate in that phase.

The rate of profit rises if capital obtains more surplus value for each dollar of wages (rate of surplus value) and falls if it needs more capital for each dollar of wages (composition). In each period, the bars show how hard each force pushed. If the bar for the rate of surplus value is taller than the fall due to the composition, the rate of profit rises; if not, it falls. This shows which countertendency sustained profit in each stage and when it stopped doing so.

:::como How is this decomposition calculated?
For each phase between the years a<sub>0</sub> and a<sub>1</sub>: Δln r = Δln e − Δln(K/V), with e = S/V and K/V taken from the section ‘The two-sided law’ (net broad surplus value of the business sector; K at current cost; V by industry from 1948 or within-industry from 1972). In the within-industry variant, K/V = (K<sub>real</sub>/H) · (P<sub>K</sub>/w): K<sub>real</sub> is K deflated by the implicit price of private non-residential fixed capital (current-cost stock over the BEA quantity index, 2017 = 1); H is the hours of productive workers (BLS) and w = V/H. The cut-off years are the five most frequent break years in the main multiverse (baseline configuration: minimum segment of 8 years, BIC), separated from each other by at least six years. The downloads include the full table (contratendencias_fases.csv).
:::

### 1. Raising of the degree of exploitation

The rate of surplus value is the direct indicator (see the section ‘The two-sided law’). Here output per hour in the non-farm business sector is compared with real compensation per hour: the gap between the two curves is the part of productivity that does not return to the workers.

If workers produce more per hour but their real wage grows less, the difference stays with capital. The gap between the two lines is that difference, accumulated since 1972.
{: .llano }

The extension of working time does not show up in the hours paid by establishments, which decline. Two indications of its displacement beyond a single job: the proportion of employed people with more than one job and that of those working part-time involuntarily. Neither measures the intensity of labour, which still has no indicator.
{: .nota }

Two signs that working time is being stretched outside the paid hours of a single job: people who need two or more jobs and people who work fewer hours than they want.

### 2. Reduction of wages below their value

The value of labour power is not directly observed; what is observed is the real wage of production and non-supervisory workers. A real wage that for decades remains below its 1972 level, while productivity multiplies, indicates a compression of wages that the rise in productivity alone does not explain.

What a production worker can buy with one hour's wage, after discounting inflation.

### 3. Cheapening of the elements of constant capital

Three indices on machines, buildings and equipment. The technical composition tells how much physical capital there is per hour of labour: it grows non-stop. The other two tell how much that capital costs compared with the wage and with consumption: they fall. Capital needs ever more means of production per worker, but each one is cheaper, and that slows the fall of the rate.
{: .llano }

[[leyenda l-ct-c]]
[[grafico c-ct-c corto]]

Indices, 1972 = 100. The technical composition grows much more than the value composition because each unit of capital becomes cheaper relative to labour.
{: .nota }

### 4. Relative surplus population

How many people are available for capital to employ: the unemployed and underemployed in the United States; in the world, the unemployed and own-account or contributing family workers. Vulnerable employment worldwide falls because hundreds of millions of people move into work as wage earners.
{: .llano }

[[leyenda l-ct-d]]
[[grafico c-ct-d]]

Relative surplus population is not only unemployment: it includes those who work on their own account or in family labour, available to be absorbed by capital. At the world scale, their reabsorption is the main source of the growth of the mass of surplus value (see the world mass in hours).
{: .nota }

### 5. Foreign trade

What share of the profits of US corporations comes from abroad, and how much trade weighs in the world economy.
{: .llano }

[[leyenda l-ct-e]]
[[grafico c-ct-e]]

**Transfer of labour time through foreign trade.** On the world market, countries with higher productivity receive more labour in exchange for less. The chart estimates how many hours of other people's labour each country or group appropriates per year: the hours embodied in what it imports minus those embodied in what it exports, in billions of hours. Positive means appropriation; negative, cession.
{: .nota }

[[leyenda l-ct-e2]]
[[grafico c-ct-e2]]

:::como How is the transfer of hours calculated?
For each country, h = hours worked in the year (persons employed × annual hours per person employed, Penn World Table 11.0) / GDP in current dollars (World Bank). The average hour content of one dollar of world exports is h<sub>X</sub> = Σ exports × h / Σ exports. A country's net transfer is imports × h<sub>X</sub> − exports × h. The countries with data on hours are used (some 120, from 2005); the income groups are those of the World Bank.

Assumptions: what a country exports embodies the average hours per dollar of its economy, and what it imports, the average content of world exports; intermediate inputs are not traced, which would require a world input–output matrix; all hours worked are counted, not only productive ones. The transfer does not create surplus value: it redistributes it among capitals and national formations.
:::

### 6. Share capital and interest-bearing capital

What share of profit goes to finance: the profits of the financial sector, the interest and dividends paid by non-financial corporations, and how many shares they issue or buy back.
{: .llano }

[[leyenda l-ct-f]]
[[grafico c-ct-f]]

Fictitious capital consists of claims on future surplus labour. The market value of the shares of non-financial corporations grows much faster than their debt and than output: its validation requires a mass of surplus value that has not yet been produced.
{: .nota }

The stock-market value of US non-financial corporations compared with the country's output, and their debt. The stock-market value goes from a third to more than twice output: these are claims that can only be paid with surplus labour that has not yet been produced.
{: .llano }

[[leyenda l-ct-f2]]
[[grafico c-ct-f2 corto]]

Negative net equity issuance means that non-financial corporations buy back more shares than they issue: they transfer profit to shareholders instead of accumulating it.
{: .nota }

:::como Where do these indicators come from?
- **Exploitation:** output per hour (OPHNFB) and real compensation per hour (COMPRNFB) of the non-farm business sector, BLS via FRED, re-indexed to 1972 = 100.
- **Wages:** average hourly earnings of production and non-supervisory workers (AHETPI, BLS) divided by the consumer price index (CPIAUCSL), in 2017 dollars. Household debt service relative to disposable income (TDSP, Federal Reserve) in the downloads.
- **Constant capital:** implicit price of non-residential investment (NIPA A008RD) over the personal consumption expenditures price index (DPCERG); technical composition and relative price as in the decomposition by phases.
- **Relative surplus population:** employment rate of people aged 25 to 54 (LNS12300060) and U-6 labour underutilisation (U6RATE), BLS; world vulnerable employment and unemployment, ILO modelled estimates published by the World Bank (SL.EMP.VULN.ZS, SL.UEM.TOTL.ZS).
- **Foreign trade:** corporate profits received from the rest of the world (NIPA B933RC) over total corporate profits (A051RC), from 1998; world trade over GDP (World Bank, NE.TRD.GNFS.ZS).
- **Extension of working time:** employed people with more than one job (LNS12026619, from 1994) and part-time for economic reasons (LNS12032194), over total employment (CE16OV), BLS.
- **Transfer through foreign trade:** see the drop-down on the transfer of hours.
- **Fictitious capital:** market value of the shares issued by non-financial corporations (Z.1, NCBEILQ027S) and their debt in securities and loans (BCNSDODNS), over GDP (GDP).
- **Financial capital:** profits of financial corporations over domestic profits (A587RC / A445RC), from 1998; net interest of non-financial corporations over their net operating surplus (B471RC / W326RC); dividends over after-tax profit (B467RC / A466RC); net equity issuance of non-financial corporations (Z.1, FU103164103) over their gross value added (A455RC).

None of these indicators by itself measures a countertendency: they are observable manifestations that must be read together with the rate and the mass of surplus value. All of them are in the downloads (contratendencias_indicadores.csv).
:::
