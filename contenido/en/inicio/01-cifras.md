Four numbers to start with: where the US rate of profit stands today compared with its historical average; how many dollars of surplus value capital obtains for each dollar it pays in wages to productive workers; how much surplus value is produced in total, measured by what it can buy; and what share of the world's capital is now in China.

:::como How are these four figures calculated?
- **Median of the multiverse.** For each year, the median of the rates of all the specifications in the main set, after dividing each one by its own period average and multiplying by 100.
- **S/V with within-industry correction.** S = value added of the business sector (BEA, A195RC) − value added of rental housing (A2009C − B701RC) − financial services paid for by households out of their wages (DFNLRC × A033RC / A065RC) − depreciation of the sector's non-residential capital − V. V = compensation of production and non-supervisory workers in the productive industries: employment × hourly wage × weekly hours × 52 by supersector (BLS, CES), scaled by the compensation/wages ratio to include benefits.
- **Mass of surplus value in purchasing power.** The same S, with V from all productive industries (by-industry criterion), deflated by the GDP deflator (2017 = 100).
- **China's share of world capital.** China's capital stock at current PPPs (cn, Penn World Table 11.0) over the sum of the 79 countries in the balanced panel (those with data since 1960).
:::
